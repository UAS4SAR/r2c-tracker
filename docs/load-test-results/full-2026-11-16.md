# Full-length load measurement, 2026-11-16

Local only. Postgres on this VM, weather stubbed, no FAA credentials, no Cloud Run. Tracker process in cgroup `r2c-loadtest`: `memory.max=1GiB`, swap max 0, `cpu.max` of one core, also pinned to one CPU. `R2C_LOADTEST_INSTRUMENT=1`. No tracker behavior change. Per-run JSON is in [full-2026-11-16/](full-2026-11-16/).

Steady runs are 360 s: the spec's 60 s warmup plus 5 measured minutes, reported together. Quiet tablets park at 30 s, so a quiet fleet's latencies are from that opening, and the rest of the 6 minutes is an idle soak. Scenario C kills the process after the first hello-acks, then keeps traffic for the remainder of the 360 s. The Android missing-ack run is 300 s with `hello_ack` delayed 12 s. Cold start is three 60 s trials.

## Headline

The global coordination lock is not the bottleneck at this load. Across 40,000 hub-lock acquires in the 16-tablet campaign, wait p99 was 0.003 ms and the maximum was 0.066 ms. The submission lock was taken only by the three flight uploads and waited about 0 ms. Event-loop lag stayed near 0.6 ms p50 and about 2 ms p99 while 48 drones sent a sighting and a traffic position every second. CPU on the one core peaked at 10% during cold start and was 6.5% for the hottest steady campaign. RSS peaked at 183 MiB. Nothing was OOM-killed under the 1 GiB cap.

What does grow is the tail of hello and heartbeat acks as more tablets connect at once, and upload time from the 6-tablet campaign to the 16-tablet one. Heartbeat p50 stays about 5–30 ms even at 48 flying tablets. The p99 tail moves into the 300–600 ms range when 80 or more sockets connect together. That tail is not lock wait.

## Scenario B

Ten orgs: 3 hot and 7 quiet. Quiet tablets parked (14). Hot tablets kept flying for the whole 360 s. One upload per hot org, weather stubbed. NOTAM calls are 503 and retry every 15 s, which is why `http_errors` is in the hundreds. Those are not tracker 500s.

Heartbeat p50 does not degrade from 18 to 48 flying tablets. It stays about 5 ms. The p99 tail does, from 341 ms to 456 ms. Upload p50 goes from 220 ms to 682 ms. The submission lock does not explain that: three acquires, all under 0.001 ms. Hub-lock p99 stays 0.003 ms at 40,000 acquires.

Both hot runs opened many more sockets than the tablet count (692 connects for 62 planned sockets at 16 tablets). Clients were reconnecting through the window. That churn is visible in the connect-failure count. It does not show up as hub-lock wait.

## Warm restart at 16 tablets

Same 48 flying tablets plus 7 quiet orgs. Restart after the first hello-acks, then the rest of the 360 s.

| Delay | Hello p50 / p95 / p99 | Time from socket close to next hello-ack p50 / p95 / p99 | Connects / failures | Lag p99 / max | Hub lock p99 / max | CPU % | RSS |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- | ---: |
| Lockstep 2/4/8/10 s | 254 / 330 / 390 | 2353 / 10066 / 10091 ms | 459 / 399 | 2.1 / 62 | 0.003 / 0.026 | 6.0 | 170 |
| Jitter uniform 0–5 s | 102 / 325 / 374 | 2155 / 4682 / 4779 ms | 950 / 892 | 2.1 / 121 | 0.003 / 0.066 | 7.0 | 167 |

Lockstep's close-to-ack p50 is 2.4 s, which is the 2 s backoff plus the ack. Its p95 is the 10 s cap, so some tablets needed another step. Jitter's p50 is similar (2.2 s) and its p95 is 4.7 s, inside the 0–5 s draw, instead of stacking on the 10 s cap. Jitter also produced more connect attempts (950 versus 459). Spreading the first retry does not by itself stop later reconnects during the five minutes.

`reconnect_from_listen` p50 is 826 ms for lockstep and 60 s for jitter. That series mixes the restart herd with any later hello during the five minutes, so the close-to-ack numbers above are the ones that describe the backoff.

## Android missing ack at 16 tablets

`hello_ack` delayed 12 s for 300 s. Zero hello-acks landed. 1,169 ack timeouts, 1,613 connects, 444 other connect failures. That is the 0-delay Android retry looping, plus iOS on the normal ladder, for 48 hot tablets and 14 quiet ones. Hub-lock p99 stayed 0.001 ms. Lag p99 1.7 ms, max 44 ms. CPU 4.9%. RSS 183 MiB, the highest in the set, still far under 1 GiB. No OOM.

## Bottleneck

At 48 simultaneous flying tablets, one sighting and one traffic position per second each, on one core and under a 1 GiB cap:

- The global `asyncio.Lock` wait is microseconds. It is not where time goes.
- The flight-submission lock is idle except for the three uploads, and those waits are ~0 ms.
- The event loop is usually under 1 ms of lag. A few spikes reach 30–150 ms. They are rare (p99 stays about 2 ms).
- The one core is under 10% busy. RSS is about 170 MiB. The 1 GiB limit was not approached.
- Latency that does move is hello-ack p50, which flattens near 270 ms once 80 sockets connect together, heartbeat p99 (hundreds of ms in a herd, about 5 ms p50 once flying), and upload p50 (220 ms at 18 flying tablets, 682 ms at 48).
- The concurrency cap of 80, not the lock, is what produces 429s. It shows up when a quiet fleet opens more than 80 sockets at once. Raising that cap was not part of this measurement.

So the next place to look, if a change is made later, is per-connection work and the heartbeat tail under a connect herd, not sharding the global lock. This run did not change that code.
